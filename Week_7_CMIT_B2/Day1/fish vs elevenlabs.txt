## Fish Audio vs ElevenLabs — Evaluation

I evaluated the current offerings, focusing especially on **Urdu and Urdu-English mixed speech**, because that is where the choice becomes more interesting.

### Executive summary

| Category                                      | Fish Audio S2-Pro                              | ElevenLabs                                    | Winner                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Latency**                                   | ~100 ms time-to-first-audio                    | ~75 ms Flash; ~280 ms v3 Conversational       | 🟢 **ElevenLabs**              |
| **Naturalness**                               | Excellent, especially multilingual             | Exceptional, particularly English             | 🟢 **ElevenLabs**              |
| **Emotion**                                   | Very strong, natural-language emotion controls | Exceptional, v3 has rich emotional/audio tags | 🟢 **ElevenLabs**              |
| **Streaming**                                 | WebSocket + ~100 ms TTFA                       | Excellent streaming + WebSocket               | 🟢 **Tie / slight ElevenLabs** |
| **Voice cloning**                             | Zero/few-shot cloning from short samples       | Excellent; Instant + Professional cloning     | 🟢 **Tie**                     |
| **Pricing**                                   | Very competitive pay-as-you-go                 | More expensive on high-quality models         | 🟢 **Fish Audio**              |
| **Multilingual**                              | 80+ languages                                  | 74 languages in v3                            | 🟢 **Fish Audio**              |
| **Urdu pronunciation**                        | Promising, but less independently validated    | Explicit Urdu support                         | 🟢 **ElevenLabs**              |
| **Urdu ↔ English switching**                  | **Very promising**                             | Very good                                     | 🟢 **Fish Audio**              |
| **Overall for a multilingual/Urdu assistant** | **9/10**                                       | 8.5/10                                        | 🏆 **Fish Audio**              |

---

# 1. Latency

This is one of Fish Audio's biggest advantages.

Fish Audio's **S2-Pro advertises ~100 ms time-to-first-audio**, which is excellent for conversational applications. ([Fish Audio][1])

ElevenLabs has several relevant models:

* **Flash v2.5:** ~75 ms
* **v3 Conversational:** ~280 ms
* **v3:** higher latency and not recommended for realtime conversations. ([ElevenLabs][2])

So:

**Pure lowest latency:** ElevenLabs Flash
**High-quality conversational latency:** Fish S2-Pro is extremely competitive.

### Winner: 🟢 ElevenLabs, narrowly

If you're building a realtime voice agent, both are fast enough. ElevenLabs Flash has the lower published number.

---

# 2. Naturalness

This is where ElevenLabs still has a reputation advantage.

ElevenLabs' current v3 is specifically designed for highly human-like speech, contextual understanding and expressive delivery. ([ElevenLabs][3])

Fish S2-Pro is also extremely strong. Fish's published benchmarks report:

* 0.99% English WER
* 0.54% Chinese WER
* 81.88% EmergentTTS-Eval win rate
* 4.51/5 quality score on its instruction benchmark. ([Fish Audio][4])

There is also an important caveat: Fish's public blind-test comparison is **Fish's own evaluation**, so I wouldn't treat it as an unbiased independent benchmark. It reports S2-Pro ahead of ElevenLabs v3 overall, but independent evidence specifically for Urdu is much thinner. ([Fish Audio][5])

### My assessment

**English narration:** ElevenLabs
**Multilingual speech:** Fish Audio
**Overall naturalness:** ElevenLabs has the safer choice.

### Winner: 🟢 ElevenLabs

---

# 3. Emotion

Both are excellent here.

Fish S2-Pro allows natural-language control such as:

> `[whispers sweetly]`

and supports expressive control rather than being restricted to a small fixed emotion vocabulary. It also supports multi-speaker dialogue. ([Fish Audio][1])

ElevenLabs v3 goes even further with tags such as:

* `[sad]`
* `[angry]`
* `[happily]`
* `[whispers]`
* `[shouts]`
* `[laughs]`
* `[sighs]`

and supports multi-speaker dialogue. ([ElevenLabs][6])

For an assistant that needs to sound emotionally expressive, ElevenLabs is still the more mature option.

### Winner: 🟢 ElevenLabs

---

# 4. Streaming

Both platforms are very good.

### Fish Audio

S2-Pro is designed around realtime generation and advertises approximately **100 ms time-to-first-audio**. Fish also provides WebSocket-based realtime streaming. ([Fish Audio][1])

### ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs supports streaming endpoints and WebSocket-based realtime conversational audio. Its Flash models are designed specifically for realtime applications. ([ElevenLabs][2])

ElevenLabs currently advertises first audio around **75 ms** for Flash/Turbo.

### Winner: 🟢 Slight ElevenLabs advantage

But in practice, network latency, chunk size and your own backend will matter substantially.

---

# 5. Voice cloning

This is basically a draw.

Fish Audio supports **zero-shot/few-shot voice cloning from short samples**. Its S1 documentation describes short-sample cloning and cross-language voice cloning. ([Fish Audio][7])

ElevenLabs has:

* Instant Voice Cloning
* Professional Voice Cloning
* multiple clone tiers depending on subscription. ([ElevenLabs][8])

ElevenLabs' cloning ecosystem is more mature and polished.

Fish's advantage is that it can be very aggressive about **short reference samples + multilingual generation**.

### Winner: 🟡 Tie

For a production product where voice identity is extremely important, I'd slightly favor ElevenLabs.

---

# 6. Pricing

This is where Fish Audio becomes very attractive.

Fish's API is pay-as-you-go with **no monthly API subscription requirement**. S1/S2-Pro are priced at:

**$15 / million UTF-8 bytes**

Fish estimates this as roughly **12 hours of speech / million bytes**, although actual usage depends on the input. ([Fish Audio][9])

Fish's current credit packages also start at:

* $9.99 → 40K credits
* $29.99 → 150K
* $79.99 → 500K
* $149.99 → 1M credits. ([Fish Audio][10])

ElevenLabs API pricing is currently:

* Flash/Turbo: **$0.05 / 1K characters**
* Multilingual v2/v3: **$0.10 / 1K characters**
* v3: **$0.10 / 1K characters**. ([ElevenLabs][11])

And the consumer plans start at:

* Free
* $6 Starter
* $22 Creator
* $99 Pro
* $299 Scale
* $990 Business. ([ElevenLabs][12])

### Winner: 🟢 Fish Audio

Especially if you're generating a **large amount of multilingual speech**.

---

# 7. Multilingual support

Fish S2-Pro officially advertises **80+ languages**. ([Fish Audio][1])

ElevenLabs v3 currently supports **74 languages**, including Urdu, Hindi, Punjabi, Sindhi, Arabic and Persian. ([ElevenLabs][13])

So both are excellent.

But Fish has an important feature for your use case: its S1 documentation explicitly describes **mixed-language prompts and cross-lingual TTS**. ([Fish Audio][7])

### Winner: 🟢 Fish Audio

Particularly for applications where users aren't speaking a single language throughout an interaction.

---

# 8. Urdu pronunciation 🇵🇰

This is the most important caveat.

### ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs **explicitly lists Urdu (`urd`) as a supported language in v3**. ([ElevenLabs][13])

So there is no question about official Urdu support.

It also has dedicated Urdu speech capabilities elsewhere in its platform. ([ElevenLabs][14])

### Fish Audio

Fish S2-Pro advertises 80+ languages, but the public S2-Pro documentation I found does **not provide the same explicit language-by-language Urdu listing** that ElevenLabs provides. ([Fish Audio][1])

Fish's multilingual architecture and mixed-language capability make Urdu **promising**, but I would not claim that Fish definitively beats ElevenLabs at Urdu pronunciation without an actual Urdu benchmark.

### Winner: 🟢 ElevenLabs — for confidence

This is an important distinction:

**ElevenLabs = officially documented Urdu support.**

**Fish = potentially excellent Urdu performance, but less publicly documented/independently validated specifically for Urdu.**

---

# 9. Urdu-English switching

This is where I would choose **Fish Audio**.

Consider a Pakistani user saying:

> "Yaar mujhe ye match ka prediction batao, and also tell me which player has the highest average."

That's very different from clean textbook Urdu or English.

Real users naturally mix:

**Urdu + English + names + technical terms + numbers.**

Fish's S1 documentation explicitly says it can mix languages in the same prompt and adapt to the script/context. ([Fish Audio][7])

ElevenLabs also handles multilingual input very well, and v3 supports Urdu and English. ([ElevenLabs][3])

But for **code-switching**, Fish's architecture and multilingual focus make it particularly attractive.

### Winner: 🟢 Fish Audio

---

# Final verdict

If your question were:

> **"What's the best general-purpose premium TTS?"**

I'd say:

### 🏆 ElevenLabs

It still has the advantage in:

* English naturalness
* emotional performance
* mature voice cloning
* production ecosystem
* documented Urdu support
* overall polish

---

But your question is specifically about **Fish Audio vs ElevenLabs**, including **Urdu pronunciation and Urdu-English switching**.

For that use case:

# 🏆 Fish Audio is the better choice.

I'd score them approximately:

| Requirement               |          Fish | ElevenLabs |
| ------------------------- | ------------: | ---------: |
| Latency                   |          9/10 | **9.5/10** |
| Naturalness               |          9/10 | **9.5/10** |
| Emotion                   |          9/10 | **9.5/10** |
| Streaming                 |    **9.5/10** |     9.5/10 |
| Voice cloning             |        9.5/10 | **9.5/10** |
| Price                     |     **10/10** |       8/10 |
| Multilingual              |    **9.5/10** |       9/10 |
| Urdu                      |         8/10* |   **9/10** |
| Urdu-English switching    |    **9.5/10** |       9/10 |
| **Your use case overall** | **9.3/10 🏆** | **9.2/10** |

*Fish's Urdu score is intentionally conservative because I couldn't find a sufficiently strong independent Urdu-specific benchmark.

### My recommendation

For an **Urdu/English AI assistant**, I'd build the first version with **Fish Audio S2-Pro**.

The combination of:

**low latency + streaming + multilingual generation + short voice cloning + emotional control + lower cost**

makes it a very compelling choice.

I'd keep **ElevenLabs as the fallback/benchmark**, particularly if your testing shows that Fish mispronounces Pakistani Urdu words, names, or Roman-Urdu/English code-switches.

**One important next step:** don't decide based purely on published specifications. For your project, the best evaluation would be a **20–30 sentence Urdu/English benchmark** containing Roman Urdu, Urdu script, English technical terms, Pakistani names, numbers, abbreviations, emotions, and code-switching. Then measure pronunciation, latency and human preference side-by-side. That would give you a much more defensible conclusion than generic TTS benchmarks.
